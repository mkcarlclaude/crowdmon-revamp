Features

Everything between a raw video and a dataset

The detector is the least interesting part, and deliberately swappable. The pipeline around it is the product.

Pre-labelled the moment you submit

Paste a link and walk away. Extraction, de-duplication and detection run unattended on a queue — there is no button anybody has to remember to press.

Verify, never draw

Accept, adjust or reject. Three keys, seconds per frame — the interaction that made hour ten cost less than hour one.

Accept Adjust Reject

Snapshots with a split manifest

Export to R2 with train/validation/test recorded, plus the inclusion policy that produced it. A frozen evaluation pool never leaks into training.

// manifest.json
{ "train": 1482,
  "val": 318, "test": 318,
  "policy": "admin_verdicts_only",
  "eval_pool": "frozen" }

Open vocabulary, no retraining

Classes are rows in a table carrying an appearance prompt. Adding a character is an edit, not a deploy — and every box is stamped with the prompt version that produced it.

class_id prompt ver
1 "a photo of paimon" v3
2 "a photo of raiden shogun" v3
3 "a photo of nahida" draft

Runs on one desktop

CPU-only inference on a machine under a desk. Bounded sampling keeps it inside a nightly window.

Safe to show strangers

Anonymous verdicts are recorded and never promoted. Frames are served by short-lived signed URLs from a curated pool.

Observable by default

Traces, metrics and logs from the worker to the edge, because nobody is watching it run.

How it works

Three steps, one of which is you

From a link to a dataset, with a human in exactly one place.

1

Submit a video

A worker pulls it, cuts ~2,700 frames, drops the near-duplicates and keeps a random 200 across the timeline.

2

The model guesses

An open-vocabulary detector proposes boxes from class prompts alone — it has never been shown these characters.

3

You rule on it

Accept, adjust or reject. Verdicts are append-only and tagged by source, then packaged into a snapshot.

frames kept per video, bounded by human throughput

FAQ

Questions worth asking

Is the detector any good?

No, and that is on purpose. It is a zero-shot bootstrap that has never seen these characters — confidences sit around 0.10–0.20 and plenty of its boxes are wrong. A model that guesses badly is still worth having, because rejecting a bad guess takes under a second and a rejection is a label too. Accuracy is not the deliverable; throughput per human minute is.

Do my verdicts on the demo change the dataset?

No. Anonymous verdicts are recorded and tagged, then excluded at snapshot time by design. Admitting untrusted labels would force consensus resolution, agreement scoring and trust weighting — all deliberately out of scope.

Can it find something the model missed entirely?

Not on its own — that is a structural limit of a verify-only interface. A frame with no prediction never enters the queue, so false negatives look identical to absence. Admins can file missing-object reports, and the report rate per class is the number that says whether a prompt is working.

Does it train anything?

Not yet. Training, a model registry and a distilled detector are all out of scope for v2. What exists today is the part that produces the data those would need — which is the part that was actually hard.

Why Genshin Impact?

It is a domain with recognisable, consistently-rendered subjects and an endless supply of source video, which makes it a good harness for the pipeline. The frames are game screenshots and the dataset is not distributed as a product.

See it get one wrong

One real frame, the detector's real output, and three buttons. It takes about ten seconds and no account.
