You just did the expensive part.

Three frames, about ten seconds. A model proposed, you ruled. That ruling is the scarce thing — models are cheap and getting cheaper, and the judgement that says which of their guesses is worth keeping is not.

crowdmon is the machinery around those ten seconds: it takes a recorded video, cuts it into frames, runs a model that has never seen these characters, and hands a person a queue. The model is deliberately mediocre and deliberately swappable. Everything around it is the thing that was actually built.

I tried this in 2023 with a tool that made you draw every box from scratch, and abandoned it — hour ten cost exactly what hour one cost. Checking is cheap. Drawing is not.

A labelled picture of a video game, and the machine that makes one cheap

Read only the left-hand notes and you will still get the whole thing.

maintainer one person status collecting compute one desk, no GPU licence see repository
In plain words

Computers only learn from examples that a human has already marked up. Making those examples is slow, dull work, and it is most of what "building an AI" actually costs.

This is a machine for making that part cheap.

1 · Motivation

Supervised object detection needs boxes drawn around things, by hand, at a rate that does not improve with practice. An earlier attempt in 2023 shipped a competent annotation tool and died of exactly that: hour ten cost what hour one cost.

The stated aim is not detector accuracy. It is throughput per human minute. A model that guesses badly still helps, because rejecting a bad guess takes under a second and a rejection is a label too.

In plain words

It watches a recording of someone playing a game, grabs still pictures from it, and throws away the ones that look the same as the last.

2 · Composition

InstanceWhat it is
imageone frame from gameplay video, 1920×1080
predictionbox, class, confidence, prompt version — immutable
verdictaccept / adjust / reject, append-only, tagged by source

Classes are rows in a table, five active at a time, each carrying the appearance prompt that produced it. Changing a prompt is an edit, not a deploy.

In plain words

Only 200 pictures are kept per video, even though there are thousands available.

The limit is deliberate: one person can only look at so many, and a pile nobody will ever get through is not an asset.

3 · Collection process

ParameterValueWhy
sample200 / videobounded by human throughput
available~2,700 / videothe rest keep their rows and wait
selectionrandom across timelineavoids clustering on one scene
hardware1 desktop CPUit is what there is
triggernoneruns on submit, unattended
In plain words

Here is the machine being wrong, which is the normal case and the reason a person is still in the loop.

It has never been shown these characters. It is working from their names alone.

4 · A worked example

Fig. 1 — frame 00250, unedited model output. Three boxes returned. The left region is claimed by both Paimon (0.20) and Raiden Shogun (0.17); the right box, Paimon (0.11), is the Traveler. Two of three labels are wrong and no confidence exceeds 0.20.

In plain words

Strangers can try the tool, but nothing a stranger clicks ever counts. Otherwise anyone could quietly poison the results.

5 · Uses and limits

PropertyState
anonymous verdictsrecorded, never promoted into the dataset
evaluation poolfrozen before it was needed — cannot be added later
missed objectsreportable; a verify-only UI cannot see them otherwise
trainingout of scope not built yet

The frozen pool costs one column now and is impossible to retrofit: every image labelled before it existed was chosen by a biased rule.
